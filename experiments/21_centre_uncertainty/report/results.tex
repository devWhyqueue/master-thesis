\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Training against centre uncertainty meets the prespecified success criterion on TCGA-UT and fails it on BRACS. On TCGA-UT, the tuned arm gains 2.34 points at five patients (95\% interval: 2.03 to 2.63), the fixed arm 0.52 points (0.44 to 0.59), and both gains shrink with the patient count, to 1.32 and 0.13 points at twenty (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}). On BRACS, the tuned arm gains 0.25 points ($-0.37$ to 0.97) and the fixed arm 0.20 ($-0.12$ to 0.70). The primary contrast therefore succeeds on one dataset only, and no claim across both cohorts is supported. The isotropic control stays below 0.25 points on both datasets at every patient count, so it accounts for at most a tenth of the tuned gain on TCGA-UT.

\noindent Unlike the directional-shrinkage result \citep{queisler2026directionalShrinkage}, the TCGA-UT success does not hinge on the interval bound: the lower bound of 2.03 points lies twice the threshold above zero. The gain is nonetheless small against the headroom of 10.66 points. No BRACS interval excludes zero at any patient count, so that dataset supports neither a gain nor a loss.

\subsection{Headroom recovery and the patient-count gap}
\label{sec:recovery-results}
On TCGA-UT, the tuned arm recovers 0.220 (0.191 to 0.251) of the pool-centre headroom at five patients and 0.265 (0.222 to 0.313) at ten; the fixed arm recovers 0.048 and 0.037 (Table~\ref{tab:shares}). Because the gain falls with the patient count, the tuned arm narrows the five-to-twenty gap from 9.41 to 8.39 points, a paired reduction of 1.02 points (0.71 to 1.34) and a share of 0.109 (0.077 to 0.140). Both single steps show reductions with intervals above zero (Figure~\ref{fig:gap}). On BRACS, recovery is 0.030 ($-0.050$ to 0.106) at five patients and 0.034 at ten, and the gap reduction of $-0.09$ points ($-0.84$ to 0.70) is indistinguishable from zero. The isotropic control reduces no TCGA-UT gap by more than 0.12 points and no BRACS gap by more than 0.24 points, with intervals that include zero except for the ten-to-twenty step on TCGA-UT.

\subsection{Comparison with the control and the other cohort-only remedies}
\label{sec:comparison-results}
On TCGA-UT, the patient directions carry almost the entire gain. Ut exceeds the isotropic control by 2.18 points at five patients (1.89 to 2.49), 1.56 at ten, and 1.23 at twenty, all with intervals above zero. On BRACS the same contrast is 0.16 points ($-0.56$ to 0.98) at five patients and 0.48 ($-0.06$ to 1.12) at twenty, so no patient-direction benefit is supported there.

The ranking against the reused remedies separates the two families of cohort-only remedy (Figure~\ref{fig:gains}). On TCGA-UT, Ut exceeds both centre-shrinkage arms at every patient count: the contrast Ut$-$At is 1.06 points at five patients (0.81 to 1.30), 1.07 at ten, and 0.91 at twenty, and Ut$-$St is 2.20 (1.91 to 2.49), 1.64, and 1.18 points. Against cohort whitening the two arms coincide: Ut$-$RWc is 0.08 points at five patients ($-0.01$ to 0.18), $-0.08$ at ten, and 0.05 at twenty, so every interval includes zero and none reaches a tenth of a point in either direction. On BRACS, Ut is indistinguishable from At, RWc, and St at every patient count, with point contrasts between $-0.73$ and 0.48 points; the widest of them, $-0.73$ against tuned radial shrinkage at five patients, has an interval from $-1.64$ to 0.17.

\subsection{Selected strengths and solver behaviour}
\label{sec:selection-results}
Validation selection behaves differently on the two datasets (Table~\ref{tab:selection}). On TCGA-UT, the tuned patient arm selected $t=16$ or $t=64$ in every one of the 90 fits and never reached the largest grid value, so the selection lies inside the grid at 16 to 64 times the modelled centre variance. The fixed arm at $t=1$ gains less than a quarter as much as the tuned arm at five patients (Ut$-$U: 1.83 points, 1.55 to 2.09), so the modelled scale $\mathbf V_G$ is far below the strength that validation prefers. On BRACS, the tuned patient arm chose $t=0$, the ordinary fit, in 2 to 6 of 30 fits per patient count and spread the remaining selections over the whole grid. The isotropic control chose the largest value $t=256$ in 13 to 27 of 30 fits on both datasets, yet its accuracy never moved from R by more than 0.21 points.

\noindent The regularization strength reached a grid boundary in a minority of fits. On TCGA-UT, the fixed arm chose the smallest value $\lambda=10^{-6}$ in 3 of 30 fits at five patients and 1 at ten, and the isotropic control chose it once at five patients; no tuned patient fit chose a boundary value, all of them selecting $\lambda\in\{0.1,1\}$. On BRACS, the largest value $\lambda=100$ was chosen by the fixed arm in 4 and 3 of 30 fits at five and ten patients, by the tuned patient arm in 8, 3, and 3 fits across the three patient counts, and by the isotropic control in 1 and 2 fits at five and ten patients.

Solver behaviour separates the two covariances. Every patient-covariance candidate converged on both datasets. Among the 9,720 candidates per dataset, 538 isotropic TCGA-UT candidates and 256 isotropic BRACS candidates stopped at the iteration limit of 10,000, all of them at $t\geq16$ on TCGA-UT (61 at $t=16$, 271 at $t=64$, 206 at $t=256$) and at $t\in\{4,16,64\}$ on BRACS (3, 239, and 14 candidates). Their gradient norms are small, between $2.4\times10^{-8}$ and $1.6\times10^{-4}$ on TCGA-UT and between $8.7\times10^{-8}$ and $5.4\times10^{-5}$ on BRACS, but the selection rule excludes every candidate that did not converge, so no arm reports one. No cohort produced a zero covariance.

\subsection{Why the isotropic control gains little}
\label{sec:isotropic-results}
The isotropic control has the same total perturbation variance as the patient covariance, but it spreads that variance over 2,560 dimensions instead of the 28 to 570 dimensions the cohort covariance spans. Its variance per dimension is therefore $r/2{,}560$ times the mean eigenvalue of $\mathbf V_G$, where $r$ is the rank: 4.7 percent at five TCGA-UT patients and 1.1 percent at five BRACS patients. With $\mathbf V_G$ replaced by $\sigma^2\mathbf I$, the quadratic term in Equation~\ref{eq:loss} becomes $\tfrac{t\sigma^2}{2}\lVert\mathbf w_k-\mathbf w_{y_i}\rVert^2$, a uniform penalty on weight differences that acts much like additional ridge regularization. The regularization grid can absorb such a penalty, which is consistent with the control gaining at most 0.21 points although it usually selects the largest strength in the grid, where its total perturbation variance is 256 times the modelled one. The control thus shows that uncertainty of the same total size, without direction, buys at most a tenth of what the patient directions buy on TCGA-UT. It does not show that any directional perturbation would help: a control matched per direction, for example random directions of the same rank, was not run.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Training against the estimated uncertainty of the class centres clears the practical threshold on TCGA-UT: 2.34 points at five patients, with an interval from 2.03 to 2.63, a recovery of 22 percent of the pool-centre headroom, and a paired reduction of the five-to-twenty patient gap by 1.02 points. The gain depends on the patient directions, since a trace-matched isotropic perturbation recovers at most a tenth of it. On BRACS the remedy recovers nothing measurable at any patient count. Of the three outcomes defined in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}, TCGA-UT shows a practical remedy and a patient-direction benefit but no remedy stronger than cohort whitening, and BRACS shows none of the three.

The most informative result is the coincidence with cohort whitening. On TCGA-UT the two arms differ by at most 0.08 points at every patient count, although whitening transforms the features before an ordinary fit and the uncertainty loss leaves the features untouched and changes the objective. Section~\ref{sec:model} shows why: whitening by $(\mathbf I+\kappa\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma})^{-1/2}$ with a ridge penalty is the same as an unwhitened fit whose penalty grows along the cohort's patient directions, and Equation~\ref{eq:loss} adds a penalty of that form inside the softmax. The two remedies are therefore two implementations of the same second-moment correction, and they reach the same accuracy. Both exceed directional shrinkage by about one point at five patients and radial shrinkage by more than two, and shrinkage of the centres is a first-moment correction that leaves the cohort's patient directions as trusted as before. On this reading, what a small cohort contributes is information about which directions not to trust rather than a better estimate of where its classes are, and acting on that information through the loss or through the features makes no measurable difference.

The strength that validation selects is the second informative result. Every TCGA-UT selection sits at 16 or 64 times $\mathbf V_G$, and the arm fixed at the modelled scale $t=1$ gains 0.52 points against the tuned arm's 2.34. The Gaussian model of Section~\ref{sec:model} therefore gets the useful direction structure right and the useful scale wrong by more than an order of magnitude. Two mechanisms are consistent with this. The cohort covariance is estimated from $C(G-1)$ deviations and is zero outside its span, so a large $t$ compensates for the directions it misses; and the bound in Equation~\ref{eq:loss} is loose, so the penalty it imposes at the modelled scale is weaker than the expected loss it stands in for. The fits do not separate these, and the selected strength has no interpretation as a calibrated variance.

All cohort-only remedies built on the same patient covariance leave most of the headroom unrecovered. On TCGA-UT, the uncertainty loss and cohort whitening recover about a fifth of it at five patients, directional shrinkage about an eighth, and radial shrinkage nothing measurable; on BRACS none recovers a measurable part. The remaining eight to nine points at five patients are not reached by estimating centres better from the cohort, nor by training against their uncertainty. This strengthens the conclusion of the preceding reports that the missing centre information must come from outside the labelled cohort.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The uncertainty model makes three assumptions that the study does not verify. It pools the between-patient covariance across classes, so all classes are assumed to share the same patient-level noise. It uses the covariance of the cohort's own patient means, which includes the finite-patch noise of each patient mean, so $\mathbf V_G$ slightly overstates the between-patient part. And it scales that covariance by $1/G$ and treats the centre error as Gaussian. The covariance is estimated from $C(G-1)$ deviations in 2,560 dimensions, only 28 for BRACS at five patients, and is zero outside its span, so most of the feature space receives no perturbation. The accuracy gain does not show that the perturbation matches the true centre error, and the selected strengths of 16 to 64 times the modelled variance show that the $1/G$ scaling does not predict what validation prefers.

The strength grid spans $t\leq256$, which the tuned patient arm never selected on TCGA-UT and selected in 6, 1, and 5 of 30 fits on BRACS, so the reported gains are not bounded by the grid on TCGA-UT. The direction-free control does drift to the largest value, so its reported effect is a grid-bounded one. The isotropic control matches only the total variance; it does not separate the effect of the specific patient directions from that of any low-rank perturbation of the same size. All arms reuse the three evaluation splits of the source studies, so they share test patients with every earlier comparison in this series, and repeated use of these partitions across studies inflates the chance that some remedy appears to work by selection alone. The results are conditional on frozen Virchow2 features, multinomial logistic regression, 160 patches per class, and the two cohorts studied; transfer to other cohorts, feature extractors, or class counts is untested.
