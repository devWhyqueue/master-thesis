\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Broader patient sampling improves accuracy at a fixed patch budget in both datasets. On BRACS, additional patches from the same patients also improve accuracy, and effective support gives the closest single-predictor description of the grid. On TCGA-UT, depth gains are small, breadth dominates, and a positive residual breadth term remains after adjustment for effective support.

\subsection{Realized allocations and accuracy grid}
Both common-pool feasibility audits passed without reducing the grid. Eligible patients per class and split range from 26 to 56 on BRACS and from 30 to 514 on TCGA-UT. These counts are identical across all three depth levels. All 135 selected fits per dataset converged, covering nine cells, three splits, and five draws. Their pooled patient-macro balanced accuracies reproduce the completed analyses. Table~\ref{tab:grid-results} reports the full grid, with each cell averaging fifteen fitted allocations.

\noindent BRACS accuracy rises from 34.55\% at $(5,8)$ to 42.88\% at $(20,32)$; TCGA-UT rises from 61.97\% to 72.08\%. Point estimates increase with both factors throughout both grids. Thus the intermediate allocation also favours breadth at this budget; the conclusion does not depend only on the two extreme cells.

\subsection{Equal-budget advantage and depth gains}
The equal-budget breadth advantage is 3.71 points on BRACS (95\% interval: 2.21 to 5.35) and 9.52 points on TCGA-UT (8.96 to 10.07). Both lower bounds exceed the one-point practical threshold. These comparisons contradict a description based only on nominal patch count within the tested allocation procedure. They compare patient composition and depth at a fixed budget, with validation-selected regularization in each cell.

\noindent BRACS depth gains remain positive at each breadth (Table~\ref{tab:contrasts-results}). At twenty patients per class, quadrupling depth improves accuracy by 2.04 points (1.13 to 2.93), establishing a gain above one point and failing the prespecified saturation criterion. On TCGA-UT, the same contrast is 0.32 points (0.24 to 0.41). This is a supported positive effect, but its entire interval lies within the practical equivalence band of $\pm1$ point. All three TCGA-UT depth-gain intervals fall inside this band. Practical saturation here means little additional accuracy over the tested depth range, not exactly zero benefit.

Breadth gains at fixed depth range from 5.75 to 6.30 points on BRACS and from 9.80 to 9.88 points on TCGA-UT, with every lower interval bound above one point. These gains also increase the patch budget fourfold. The equal-budget contrast is therefore the stronger evidence that allocation over patients matters beyond the number of labelled patches.

\subsection{Effective support and residual breadth}
The mean projected intraclass correlation is 0.130 on BRACS and 0.457 on TCGA-UT. Mean design effects consequently rise from 1.91 to 5.02 over depths 8--32 on BRACS, and from 4.20 to 15.17 on TCGA-UT. At $G=20$, class-averaged effective support rises from 86.94 to 140.26 on BRACS, but only from 38.92 to 43.58 on TCGA-UT. The correlation model therefore predicts a much weaker return from additional depth in TCGA-UT, consistent with its small observed depth gains.

\noindent On BRACS, effective support gives the smallest residual standard deviation among the three single-predictor models: 0.30 points, compared with 0.98 for breadth alone and 1.32 for nominal support (Table~\ref{tab:surface-results}). Adding breadth to nominal support leaves $\gamma_n=2.86$ points per log unit (1.74 to 4.07). Adding it to effective support instead gives $\gamma_e=0.13$ ($-1.60$ to 1.85). This residual is compatible with zero, but its interval extends outside the practical band of approximately $\pm1.44$ points per log unit. The BRACS grid is therefore well described by effective support without establishing that its residual breadth effect is practically negligible.

\noindent On TCGA-UT, breadth alone fits slightly more closely than effective support (residual SD 0.18 versus 0.25 points); nominal support fits poorly at 3.12 points. The residual after effective adjustment is $\gamma_e=4.30$ points per log unit (3.56 to 4.99), equivalent to 2.98 points (2.47 to 3.46) for a doubling of breadth at fixed effective support in the fitted model. Its lower bound exceeds the practical threshold. This supports a residual breadth association beyond this particular scalar adjustment. The grid contains no exact matched-effective-support intervention, so that conditional coefficient remains a model-based summary rather than a direct matched comparison.

The reciprocal mean correlations place the approximate transition depths near 7.7 on BRACS and 2.2 on TCGA-UT. Both are at or below the smallest depth, but the BRACS gain shows that this transition is not a sharp accuracy plateau. Effective support still increases by about 61\% from depth 8 to 32 on BRACS, compared with 12\% on TCGA-UT. A design-effect transition alone does not imply that accuracy has saturated.

\subsection{Probability quality and class-specific recall}
The secondary endpoints move with the accuracy surface rather than against it. At the equal budget of 160 patches per class, the broad allocation lowers macro negative log-likelihood by 0.113 nats on BRACS (95\% interval: 0.081 to 0.145) and by 0.463 nats on TCGA-UT (0.446 to 0.482), and lowers expected calibration error by 9.39 points on BRACS (4.07 to 12.67) and 6.59 points on TCGA-UT (5.96 to 7.35). Patch-micro balanced accuracy rises by 3.99 points (2.64 to 5.13) and 9.55 points (9.01 to 10.10), close to the corresponding patient-macro gains of 3.71 and 9.52 points. The equal-budget conclusion therefore does not depend on aggregating patches within patients first.

\noindent Depth behaves as it does for accuracy. At twenty patients per class, quadrupling depth lowers BRACS macro negative log-likelihood by 0.107 nats and leaves its calibration error within 0.25 points, whereas the TCGA-UT changes are 0.009 nats and 0.29 points. The two cohorts differ in how the narrow allocations respond to depth: BRACS calibration error at five patients per class rises from 12.87\% at $m=8$ to 19.05\% at $m=32$, while the corresponding TCGA-UT values fall from 16.53\% to 11.91\%. Additional patches from five patients thus sharpen confidence without matching accuracy gains on BRACS. Calibration error over ten equal-width bins is unstable at these classwise sample sizes, so this contrast is weaker evidence than the negative log-likelihood values beside it.

Class-specific patient-macro recalls (Table~\ref{tab:class-recalls}) show that the equal-budget advantage is not carried by a single class. On TCGA-UT, all 30 cancer types gain, from 1.44 points for pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma to 19.27 points for uterine carcinosarcoma, and every lower interval bound exceeds zero. On BRACS, six of seven lesion classes gain, led by invasive carcinoma at 9.09 points (6.46 to 11.71); atypical ductal hyperplasia is the sole negative estimate at $-2.29$ points ($-5.54$ to 0.87) and its interval contains zero. Each cell's class recalls average to its reported balanced accuracy by construction, so these entries decompose the primary endpoint rather than adding an independent test.

\subsection{Split consistency and draw variation}
Equal-budget breadth gains are positive in every split, ranging from 3.49 to 3.99 points on BRACS and from 8.73 to 10.37 on TCGA-UT (Table~\ref{tab:split-results}). Depth gains at twenty patients range from 1.70 to 2.60 points on BRACS and from 0.27 to 0.37 on TCGA-UT. These split-level results preserve the pooled contrast between useful additional depth on BRACS and small depth gains on TCGA-UT.

These are descriptive dispersions over five training draws, not intervals for a fresh training cohort; the paired-bootstrap intervals above describe held-out patient variation conditional on those draws.

Class correlations are reported in Table~\ref{tab:class-iccs}. BRACS ranges from 0.077 for usual ductal hyperplasia to 0.252 for invasive carcinoma; TCGA-UT ranges from 0.314 for lung squamous cell carcinoma to 0.641 for kidney renal clear cell carcinoma. These estimates characterize dependence along the leading principal direction, not all morphological heterogeneity. Every prespecified endpoint of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} is reported: the primary accuracy surface above, and the probability-quality, patch-micro, and class-specific summaries of Table~\ref{tab:secondary-results} and Table~\ref{tab:class-recalls}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
At the tested patch budget, allocating material across more patients is beneficial in both cohorts. Equal contributions remove unequal loss weight as an explanation, while common eligibility pools and nested depth samples prevent depth from changing the selected patients. The remaining contrast concerns how much additional evidence comes from more material from the same patients and how much comes from a broader patient sample. BRACS benefits from both; TCGA-UT gains substantially more from breadth, with less than half a point from quadrupling depth at any tested breadth.

The BRACS findings make redundancy-adjusted effective support a useful descriptive summary. Its fit is substantially closer than nominal support or breadth alone, and adding a residual breadth term gives little improvement. Nevertheless, the uncertainty interval does not establish practical equivalence to zero. It would therefore be premature to conclude that redundancy fully explains the established deficit, or that deeper sampling can generally replace additional patients. Even within this experiment, the direct equal-budget comparison favours breadth by more than two points at its lower interval bound.

TCGA-UT combines high projected within-patient dependence, small depth gains, and a positive residual breadth coefficient after effective-support adjustment. This combination shows that the chosen scalar adjustment does not fully summarize the accuracy surface. It is compatible with additional patients supplying conditions absent from the observed ones, but also with dependence missed by the projection or misspecification of the log-linear surface. The result does not establish which morphological or acquisition factors matter, nor that no amount of additional depth could ever compensate for patient shortage.

For dataset construction, the supported implication is restricted but concrete: within these common eligible pools, twenty patients contributing eight patches each outperform five contributing 32 each. The contrast holds across all fixed splits and both cohorts. Extending that recommendation to other tissue-availability distributions, representations, or budgets requires further allocation experiments. Multiple feature projections and repeated cohorts would help determine whether the residual breadth term reflects limitations of the redundancy measure or patient variation that requires a richer account.
