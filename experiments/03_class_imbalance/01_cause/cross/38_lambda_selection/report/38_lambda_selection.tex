\begin{abstract}
\noindent In the matched ten-patient regime at an imbalance ratio of $\rho=100$, prior-only damage with tuned regularization is 4.21 points of patient-macro balanced accuracy on BRACS and 1.23 points on TCGA-UT, a gap of 2.97 points. This study asked how much of that gap comes from how the regularization strength $\lambda$ is selected, rather than from the prior mechanism itself. The stored $\lambda$ grids of the joint-mechanism pilot were re-predicted under a cross-fit test-set oracle, a naive test-set oracle and a fixed-$\lambda$ control, without new fits. A pre-registered zero-fit gate required the cross-fit oracle to close at least half of the gap. It closed 0.35 of it; the reduction of 1.04 points has a 95\% interval of $-0.48$ to 2.93 points. The naive oracle closed 0.19. The outcome is labelled \emph{prior gap intrinsic}: even the best $\lambda$ chosen on test patients leaves most of the BRACS--TCGA-UT prior-only gap in place. No confirmatory fits were run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and design}
\label{sec:design}
The joint-mechanism study found that the prior-only gap between BRACS and TCGA-UT depends strongly on regularization \citep{queisler2026jointMechanism}. With each arm's $\lambda$ selected on validation patients, prior-only damage was 4.21 points on BRACS and 1.23 points on TCGA-UT. With every arm held at the balanced arm's $\lambda$, it rose to 11.83 and 10.52 points, and the gap shrank to 1.31 points. Tuning therefore rescues seven to nine points on both datasets, but more on TCGA-UT. This raises a narrower question: is the tuned BRACS gap an artefact of a poor validation choice of $\lambda$ on BRACS's small validation set, so that a better choice would remove it?

The regime was the native setting of the joint-mechanism study: frozen Virchow2 features \citep{zimmermann2024virchow2}, logistic regression, 10 patients per class, $\rho=100$, three splits and pilot draws 0--1. Four arms were fitted per draw: balanced (B), prior-only (P), support-only (S) and combined (R). Damage is the drop from B, for example $D_P = B - P$. Every candidate on the $\lambda$ grid ($10^{-8}$ to $10^{2}$ in powers of ten) was stored, so each selection rule only re-predicts stored models. Four rules chose one $\lambda$ per arm and fit:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{tuned}: the validation-selected $\lambda$ of the joint-mechanism study;
  \item \emph{cross-fit oracle}: the test patients are split into two class-stratified halves with a fixed seed; $\lambda$ is picked on one half and scored on the other, then the halves swap and the predictions are pooled;
  \item \emph{naive oracle}: $\lambda$ is picked and scored on the same test patients;
  \item \emph{fixed}: every arm uses the balanced arm's tuned $\lambda$.
\end{itemize}
\noindent The naive oracle is optimistic by construction, because selection and evaluation share the same noise \citep{cawley2010overfitting}. The cross-fit oracle removes this winner's curse and was fixed as the primary rule. Both oracles are upper bounds on what $\lambda$ selection could achieve; neither is a deployable procedure. The oracle applies to both B and P, so each arm gets its best achievable $\lambda$.

With $g_r = D_P^{\text{BRACS}} - D_P^{\text{TCGA-UT}}$ the prior-only gap under rule $r$, the quantities of interest are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta = g_{\text{tuned}} - g_{\text{oracle}},
  \qquad
  \text{closure} = \frac{\Delta}{g_{\text{tuned}}}.
\end{equation}
\noindent Inference is on $\Delta$, not on the ratio, because a per-replicate ratio is unstable when a split's tuned gap is near zero (split~2: 0.07 points). Intervals are 95\% percentile intervals over 2,000 bootstrap replicates of the joint-mechanism study's pooled accuracy. The label rules were fixed before any rule was evaluated: \emph{no prior gap} if the $g_{\text{tuned}}$ interval includes 0; otherwise \emph{prior gap intrinsic} if the $\Delta$ interval includes 0; otherwise \emph{selection explains} for closure $\ge 0.5$ and \emph{selection contributes} below it.

Two zero-fit gates preceded a planned confirmatory run on draws 2--9. The \emph{integrity} gate required the re-derived tuned and fixed $D_P$ to reproduce the stored joint-mechanism values to $10^{-4}$ points. The \emph{closure} gate required cross-fit closure $\ge 0.5$ on draws 0--1. Any failure stops the study before the confirmatory fits.

\section{Gate outcome}
\label{sec:results}
The integrity gate passed on both datasets: the re-derived tuned and fixed $D_P$ matched the stored values to within $10^{-14}$ points. The closure gate failed (Table~\ref{tab:rules}). The cross-fit oracle reduced the gap from 2.97 to 1.93 points, a closure of 0.35. The interval of $\Delta$, $-0.48$ to 2.93 points, includes zero, so the label is \emph{prior gap intrinsic}. The confirmatory array was not submitted, and no threshold was changed after the outcome.

\noindent Three details qualify the headline number. First, the whole change occurs on BRACS: TCGA-UT prior-only damage is 1.23 points under tuning and under either oracle, so its validation-selected $\lambda$ is already near the best choice. Second, on BRACS the cross-fit reduction is not mainly a better prior-only model. Balanced accuracy of arm B fell from 39.21 to 38.55 points under the cross-fit oracle, while arm P rose from 35.01 to 35.39 points. About two thirds of the 1.05-point drop in BRACS $D_P$ therefore comes from a weaker balanced reference. Selecting $\lambda$ on half of BRACS's small test set is noisy, and that noise costs arm B more than arm P. Third, an oracle applied to P alone, with B kept at its tuned $\lambda$, reduced the gap by only 0.32 points [$-0.38$, 1.15], a closure of 0.11.

The naive oracle closed less than the cross-fit oracle, although it can only raise accuracy. It raised both arms on BRACS (B by 0.24, P by 0.81 points), which lowers damage less than the cross-fit oracle's loss in B. This ordering is a further sign that the cross-fit reduction reflects noise in arm B rather than a recoverable gain in arm P.

\section{Interpretation}
\label{sec:interpretation}
The BRACS--TCGA-UT prior-only gap is not a product of poor $\lambda$ selection on BRACS. Even when $\lambda$ is chosen with access to test labels, at most about a third of the gap disappears, and that third is not distinguishable from zero. The best-case reading for the selection explanation, the P-only oracle, closes about a tenth. Under tuned regularization, BRACS pays about three points more for class-prior imbalance than TCGA-UT, and the regularization path of the stored models cannot remove this difference.

This result complements the joint-mechanism finding. At fixed $\lambda$ the gap is small, 1.31 points, but only because both datasets then suffer seven to nine points of prior-only damage. Tuning rescues more on TCGA-UT than on BRACS, and no rule within the stored grid gives BRACS the same rescue. The gap is thus a difference in how much regularization can compensate for a shifted class prior, not a difference in how well $\lambda$ is picked. This is consistent with BRACS's smaller class margin \citep{queisler2026classProperties}, although this study does not test that link.

Two limits apply. The study re-used the pilot draws 0--1 of the joint-mechanism study; it adds no fresh data, and draws 2--9 remain untouched \citep{dwork2015reusable}. The oracle also searches only the stored grid of one model family, so a different regularizer or classifier could behave differently. The support-only gap (4.22 points tuned, 4.27 points fixed) was recorded but not analysed; it is out of scope and remains unexplained after the coverage study \citep{queisler2026supportCoverage}. For the thesis, the prior-only gap is best described as intrinsic to the prior channel under the logistic-regression regime.
