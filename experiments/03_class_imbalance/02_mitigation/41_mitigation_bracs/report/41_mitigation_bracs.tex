\begin{abstract}
\noindent Class prevalence alone costs 7.65 points of balanced accuracy on BRACS at an imbalance ratio of 100 under a frozen Virchow2 encoder and a logistic-regression readout. This report asks how much of that damage six established mitigation methods give back once the encoder adapts through low-rank updates (LoRA). On the 30 split-draw shards of the prevalence study, the unmitigated cross-entropy (CE) model is trained on a balanced allocation and, together with every method over a grid of strengths, on allocations with ratios 50 and 100 and with BRACS's native class shares; each method's strength is selected per shard on validation patients only. Under LoRA, CE loses 4.95 points at ratio 100 and 3.54 points at ratio 50, while at native shares its 0.85-point loss cannot be distinguished from zero. At ratio 100, six of eight method variants meet the pre-stated recovery criterion of at least one point with an interval above zero. A curriculum of data augmentation (CUDA) recovers the most, 2.59 points or about half of the damage, followed by train-time and post-hoc logit adjustment at 1.96 and 1.94 points, or about two-fifths; the two logit-adjustment forms are the only methods that lift tail-class recall substantially. No paired share interval reaches full recovery. CUDA is also the only method that gains at native shares, by 1.21 points against a damage that cannot be distinguished from zero, so part of its recovery likely reflects augmentation that helps regardless of imbalance. Apart from CUDA, methods that re-fit only the classifier on a frozen encoder do as well as methods trained end to end. The smaller LoRA damage reflects a balanced model about three points less accurate than the frozen readout, not a more robust imbalanced one.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}
The prevalence study of this series isolates class prevalence from patient shortage at fixed patient support. On BRACS it finds 7.65 points of balanced-accuracy damage at an imbalance ratio of $\rho=100$, against 2.91 points on TCGA-UT under the same design \citep{queisler2026prevalenceBracs,queisler2026prevalenceTcga}. A follow-up split the BRACS damage into a prior channel and a support channel of similar size and found that post-hoc logit adjustment on the frozen readout recovers only about a quarter of it \citep{queisler2026causeBracs}. Both studies keep the encoder frozen, so neither tests the established long-tailed methods that also shape the representation.

A companion report defines six such methods for patch classification under a Virchow2 encoder adapted only through LoRA: balanced sampling and a curriculum of data augmentation (CUDA) at the data level, logit adjustment and DisAlign at the algorithm level, and classifier re-training (cRT) and Gaussian clouded logit (GCL) adjustment as hybrids \citep{queisler2026mitigationMethods}. For the other damage source this series studies, patient shortage, six earlier mitigation studies found that none of their corrections worked on BRACS \citep{queisler2026patientShortage}. This report asks: \emph{once the encoder can adapt, how much of BRACS's prevalence damage does each method recover, and does recovery differ between methods trained end to end and methods that freeze the encoder and re-fit only the classifier?}

\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}
The shard design is that of the prevalence study \citep{queisler2026prevalenceBracs}: three fixed patient-disjoint BRACS splits, each drawn ten times, with $G=10$ patients per lesion class sampled from those with at least 160 training patches and a total budget of $T=7\cdot G\cdot 32=2{,}240$ patches held fixed across all allocations of a draw. Every allocation reuses the prevalence study's patient draws and class-rank permutation, so the 30 split-draw shards differ from that study only in the classifier. Training reads decoded patch images, because every method adapts the encoder: a rank-8 LoRA update on the attention projections plus a linear head (a cosine head for GCL), as defined by \citet{queisler2026mitigationMethods}.

\subsection{Allocations and methods}
Each shard's patch budget is divided among the seven classes in four ways, all taken from the prevalence study's exponential allocation profile. The \emph{balanced} allocation ($\rho=1$) trains only CE, which serves as the \emph{undamaged reference}. Three imbalanced allocations, with ratios $\rho=50$ and $\rho=100$ and with BRACS's native class shares (N, realized ratio about 6.7), each train CE as their \emph{damaged reference} and every mitigation method over the grid of Table~\ref{tab:grid}. The two ratios show whether recovery grows with severity; the native shares ask whether mitigation matters at the imbalance BRACS actually has.

Ratio 100 is close to the most severe imbalance this design can express. At $\rho=100$ the rarest class receives about 12 of the 2,240 patches, roughly one per patient. Beyond $\rho\approx125$ it would receive fewer patches than its ten patients, so the tail class could no longer draw on all its patients and the design would mix prevalence with patient shortage.

Methods fall into two training families. \emph{End-to-end} methods (balanced sampling, CUDA, train-time logit adjustment, GCL) train the LoRA update and head together. CUDA \citep{ahn2023cuda} augments image patches before the encoder, with a per-class strength that rises as the current model learns the class, so it can only act end to end. \emph{Classifier-only} methods freeze an end-to-end model's encoder and re-fit only classifier parameters: post-hoc logit adjustment, DisAlign, and cRT start from the allocation's CE model, and GCL's classifier-only form, its second training stage, starts from the end-to-end GCL model of the same $\sigma$. Logit adjustment and GCL therefore each appear once per family, giving eight method variants.

\subsection{Training length and fit count}
End-to-end training length was fixed before the grid by a pilot: CE on the balanced and the $\rho=100$ allocation, one draw per split, trained for 150, 300, 600, and 1,200 steps. The rule selects the smallest step count within 0.5 points of the best mean validation balanced accuracy at $\rho=100$. The four candidates reached 31.87, 34.22, 34.93, and 33.42 percent, so 600 steps (batch 256, learning rate $10^{-4}$, cosine schedule) were used for all end-to-end fits; classifier-only fits train 200 steps. One length serves all allocations, so that a recovery difference between allocations cannot come from training length. It was chosen at the most severe allocation and not re-tuned at $\rho=50$ or native shares. Per shard, the grid holds 35 end-to-end and 38 classifier-only configurations, for 1,050 and 1,140 fits over the 30 shards. Every configured fit completed and entered the analysis.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
The primary endpoint is test patient-macro balanced accuracy (BA), the mean of per-class patch recalls in which every test patient of a class carries equal weight \citep{queisler2026prevalenceBracs}. Estimates pool the 30 shards with equal weight per split. Uncertainty combines Bayesian bootstrap weights on test patients \citep{rubin1981bayesian} with resampling of draws within each split over 2,000 replicates. All allocations and methods share the replicate weights, so every contrast is paired; intervals are 95 percent percentile intervals.

For each method, shard, and imbalanced allocation, the grid value with the best \emph{validation} BA is selected; the test partition is never read for selection. With $m^{*}$ the selected fit and $a$ the allocation,
\begin{equation}
  D_a=\mathrm{BA}(\mathrm{CE},1)-\mathrm{BA}(\mathrm{CE},a),\qquad
  R_m=\mathrm{BA}(m^{*},a)-\mathrm{BA}(\mathrm{CE},a),\qquad
  \text{share}_m=R_m/D_a ,
  \label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
\noindent where the share is computed per replicate. A method \emph{recovers} a material part of the damage if $R_m\geq1$ point and its interval lies above zero. It shows \emph{full recovery} if the interval of $\mathrm{BA}(m^{*},a)$ contains the point estimate of $\mathrm{BA}(\mathrm{CE},1)$. Both thresholds were fixed before the fits.

Secondary readings are descriptive: head, body, and tail recall by assigned class rank; macro negative log-likelihood (NLL) and expected calibration error (ECE), raw and after validation temperature scaling, following the prevalence study's recipe; the frequency of each selected strength; and the mean share of the end-to-end and classifier-only families. The prevalence study's frozen-readout damage uses the same test patients but a different classifier, so it serves as an external reference without a paired interval.
