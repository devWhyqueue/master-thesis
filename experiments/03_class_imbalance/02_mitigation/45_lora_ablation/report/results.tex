\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Table~\ref{tab:contrast} reports the six primary contrasts at $\rho=100$. Only two of the six contrasts show no material loss, so omitting LoRA is not supported across the tested scope; each contrast still stands as an individual outcome.
The CE controls reach a balanced accuracy of 31.17 (frozen) and 31.57 (adapted) on BRACS, and 67.32 (frozen) and 69.95 (adapted) on TCGA-UT.

The CE controls explain much of this pattern. Frozen minus adapted CE is $-0.40$ points $[-1.45, 0.56]$ on BRACS but $-2.64$ points $[-3.12, -2.16]$ on TCGA-UT, so on TCGA-UT LoRA already improves the unmitigated model by more than one point. Each contrast $\delta$ equals this CE gap plus the difference between the two representations' gains over their CE controls (Table~\ref{tab:secondary}). Both forms of LA gain about twice as much without LoRA as with it: 4.58 and 4.17 against 1.96 and 1.94 points on BRACS, and 3.52 and 3.51 against 1.54 and 1.41 on TCGA-UT. On BRACS, this larger gain outweighs the small CE gap, so both LA contrasts show no material loss. On TCGA-UT, it closes most of the 2.64-point gap but leaves both lower bounds below $-1$. CUDA gains about the same in both representations (1.83 against 2.59 points on BRACS, 0.89 against 1.02 on TCGA-UT), so its contrasts largely inherit the CE gap: the TCGA-UT LoRA advantage of 2.77 points is close to the CE gap of 2.64.

Tail recall shows where the LA gains come from. In the third of classes with the smallest allocation, frozen CE recalls 2.2\% on BRACS and 53.6\% on TCGA-UT, against 5.1\% and 61.2\% with LoRA. Both forms of LA raise frozen tail recall to 23--24\% on BRACS, above their adapted values, and to 68\% on TCGA-UT, one to two points below them. The frozen head third pays for this: its recall falls from 57.0\% to 39.5--40.8\% on BRACS and from 79.6\% to 72.5--72.6\% on TCGA-UT. Frozen CUDA lifts BRACS tail recall only to 6.0\%; its BRACS gain lies in the middle third, whose recall rises from 21.4\% to 39.2\%. On TCGA-UT, frozen CUDA raises tail recall to 63.3\% but lowers head recall to 72.4\%, which leaves a net gain below one point.

Validation selects stronger corrections without LoRA. On BRACS, frozen train-time LA selects the full correction $\tau=1$ in 25 of 30 shards (adapted: 23), post-hoc LA selects it in 21 (adapted: 15), and frozen CUDA selects $p_{\mathrm{aug}}=1$ in 25 (adapted: 14). On TCGA-UT, both forms of frozen LA select $\tau=0.5$ in 19 of 30 shards (adapted: 17 and 20). Frozen CUDA selects $p_{\mathrm{aug}}=0.5$ or 1 in 27 of 30, whereas the adapted runs select 0.25 in 17 and never select 1. Two frozen BRACS selections therefore sit at the upper grid edge in most shards. For CUDA, $p_{\mathrm{aug}}=1$ is the largest possible probability; for train-time LA, values of $\tau$ above 1 remain untested. The grids were fixed before test results and were not extended.
