\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Damage under LoRA}
Imbalance costs the LoRA model between a third and a half of what it costs the frozen readout. The balanced CE model reaches 71.51 percent BA $[70.25, 72.66]$. It loses $D=0.67$ points $[0.34, 1.04]$ at $\rho=50$, $1.56$ points $[1.17, 1.94]$ at $\rho=100$, and $0.42$ points $[0.06, 0.79]$ at native shares. All three intervals exclude zero, but only the damage at $\rho=100$ reaches one point. The frozen readout lost 1.85, 2.91, and 1.18 points on the same shards \citep{queisler2026prevalenceTcga}. Two differences account for the smaller loss under LoRA. Its balanced model is 0.55 points less accurate than the frozen readout (71.51 versus 72.06 percent), while its imbalanced models are more accurate (70.84 versus 70.21 at $\rho=50$, 69.95 versus 69.14 at $\rho=100$, and 71.09 versus 70.88 at N). On BRACS, only the first difference was present.

\subsection{Recovery per method}
Figure~\ref{fig:recovery} and Table~\ref{tab:recovery} show each method's paired recovery. At $\rho=100$, three of eight variants achieve material recovery: train-time logit adjustment (1.54 points $[1.21, 1.87]$), post-hoc logit adjustment (1.41 $[1.18, 1.65]$), and CUDA (1.02 $[0.70, 1.34]$). Both forms of logit adjustment recover essentially all of the damage. Their recovery shares are 0.99 $[0.80, 1.24]$ and 0.91 $[0.75, 1.13]$, respectively. The train-time model reaches 71.49 percent BA, versus 71.51 for balanced CE. End-to-end GCL (0.93 points), DisAlign (0.83), classifier-only GCL (0.80), balanced sampling (0.70), and cRT (0.68) recover less than one point, with intervals above zero. Their recovery shares range from 0.44 to 0.60, with intervals below 1.

At $\rho=50$, no method achieves material recovery: all observed recoveries remain below one point. Both forms of logit adjustment, CUDA, and DisAlign recover 0.68 to 0.92 points, matching the damage: their recovery shares range from 1.01 to 1.37, with intervals that include 1. These shares are imprecise because the denominator is small. At native shares, CUDA gains 1.01 points $[0.63, 1.41]$, more than twice the damage, and reaches 72.10 percent BA, above balanced CE. The other recoveries remain below 0.6 points. cRT and classifier-only GCL lower accuracy by 0.21 and 0.45 points, with recovery intervals of $[-0.39, -0.05]$ and $[-0.87, -0.01]$, respectively. Every variant meets the full-recovery criterion at every allocation. This criterion does not discriminate here: the unpaired accuracy intervals span about 2.4 points, wider than any of the damage estimates.

\subsection{Imbalance-specific recovery}
Balanced gains differ in direction (Table~\ref{tab:specific}). CUDA reaches 72.32 percent BA on the balanced allocation, a gain of $G=0.81$ points $[0.48, 1.14]$ over CE. End-to-end GCL ($-0.55$ $[-0.87, -0.24]$), its classifier-only form ($-0.60$ $[-0.94, -0.27]$), and cRT ($-0.41$ $[-0.54, -0.28]$) lower BA on the balanced allocation, all with intervals below zero. DisAlign is indistinguishable from CE.

After subtracting its balanced gain, CUDA corrects none of the imbalance. Its imbalance-specific recovery is 0.20 points $[-0.17, 0.57]$ at $\rho=100$, 0.03 at $\rho=50$, and 0.20 at native shares, all with intervals that include zero. Methods with a negative balanced gain correct more than their recovery against CE suggests. At $\rho=100$, end-to-end GCL corrects 1.48 points $[1.03, 1.96]$, a share of 0.95 $[0.72, 1.21]$; classifier-only GCL corrects 1.40 points $[0.87, 1.90]$, a share of 0.90 $[0.63, 1.13]$; and cRT corrects 1.10 points $[0.85, 1.34]$, a share of 0.71. All three achieve material imbalance-specific recovery. DisAlign's recovery remains 0.83 points. Five variants therefore meet the material-recovery criterion at $\rho=100$ after subtracting balanced gains: both forms of logit adjustment, whose $G_m$ is zero, both forms of GCL, and cRT. CUDA no longer meets the criterion.

\subsection{End-to-end versus classifier-only training}
The two matched pairs behave differently. Post-hoc logit adjustment, applied to the CE model at inference without retraining, recovers nearly as much as its train-time form (1.41 versus 1.54 points at $\rho=100$, 0.92 versus 0.86 at $\rho=50$). Re-fitting GCL's classifier recovers less than end-to-end GCL: recovery falls from 0.93 to 0.80 points at $\rho=100$ and from 0.53 to 0.07 at $\rho=50$. At $\rho=100$, mean recovery shares are 0.67 for end-to-end and 0.60 for classifier-only methods, or 0.70 and 0.63 when both GCL variants are excluded. The family shares differ by 0.08 with GCL included and 0.07 with GCL excluded, calculated from unrounded estimates. These family and pair comparisons are point estimates without intervals.

\subsection{Recovery by class rank}
At $\rho=100$, CE retains 61.2 percent recall in the tail third of classes by assigned rank, versus 71.6 percent on the balanced allocation, and gains 6.3 points in the head (Figure~\ref{fig:rank}). Logit adjustment restores most of the tail recall, reaching 69.4 percent (train-time) and 69.9 percent (post hoc), at a cost of about four points of head recall. End-to-end GCL and cRT raise tail recall to about 66 percent, and DisAlign and balanced sampling to about 65 percent. CUDA does not rebalance recall: it raises head and body recall by 0.7 points and tail recall by 1.7 points, leaving the latter at 62.9 percent. Classifier-only GCL raises tail recall to 73.3 percent, above the balanced model's 71.6 percent, while head recall falls to 68.7 percent, versus 71.3 percent on the balanced allocation. Rank-group recalls are point estimates.

\subsection{Selected strengths}
Validation selection favours mild corrections. At $\rho=100$, train-time logit adjustment selects half the prior correction, $\tau=0.5$, in 17 of 30 shards; its post-hoc form selects this value in 20 of 30. The full correction $\tau=1$ is selected in 9 and 10 shards, respectively, and $\tau=0.25$ in 4 and none. CUDA selects its lowest augmentation probability, $p_{\mathrm{aug}}=0.25$, in 17 of 30 shards and never selects $p_{\mathrm{aug}}=1$. On the balanced allocation, it selects 0.25 in 20 of 30 shards. End-to-end GCL selects $\sigma=0.05$ in 18 of 30 shards; its classifier-only form selects the lowest value, $\sigma=0.02$, in 29. DisAlign selects $\gamma=1.5$ in 19 shards, and balanced sampling selects full balancing ($s=1$) in 19. Selections at $\rho=50$ resemble those at $\rho=100$, except that DisAlign splits evenly between $\gamma=1.5$ and its largest value, $\gamma=2$ (14 shards each). At native shares, DisAlign selects its largest value, $\gamma=3$, in 16 of 30 shards. Train-time logit adjustment selects all three values (9, 8, and 13 shards).

\subsection{Probability quality}
Imbalance does not worsen the LoRA model's raw probabilities, which are overconfident at every allocation. Balanced CE has a raw NLL of 1.37 nats and a raw ECE of 16.8 points; at $\rho=100$, these values are 1.32 nats and 16.0 points. Temperature scaling removes most of this miscalibration and reveals a small cost of imbalance: scaled NLL rises from 1.03 to 1.07 nats and scaled ECE from 2.3 to 3.4 points. At $\rho=100$, CUDA has by far the lowest raw NLL, 1.08 nats $[1.02, 1.15]$, and raw ECE, 11.5 points $[10.5, 12.6]$. cRT (1.48 nats) and balanced sampling (1.43 nats) have the highest raw NLL. After scaling, NLL ranges from 0.98 (CUDA) to 1.07 nats across methods. Scaled ECE ranges from 1.4 points (classifier-only GCL) to 2.7 points (balanced sampling and DisAlign), versus 3.4 for CE. The scaled intervals are unpaired and overlap. CUDA's augmentation curbs raw overconfidence, but temperature scaling brings every other method close to it.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{How much does each method recover?} On TCGA-UT, logit adjustment closes the gap. At $\rho=100$, both forms recover the full 1.56 points within the uncertainty of the paired recovery share and restore tail recall from 61 to almost 70 percent. No other method recovers the full damage: CUDA, both forms of GCL, DisAlign, balanced sampling, and cRT recover between two-fifths and two-thirds of it. At $\rho=50$, all differences between methods remain below the one-point practical threshold. At native shares, differences reach 1.46 points between CUDA and classifier-only GCL, despite damage of only 0.42 points.

\textbf{Is CUDA's gain an imbalance correction?} No. CUDA improves the balanced model by 0.81 points, comparable to its recoveries of 0.84, 1.02, and 1.01 points at $\rho=50$, $\rho=100$, and native shares. Its imbalance-specific recovery is indistinguishable from zero at every allocation, and it leaves the gap between head and tail recall as wide as under CE. It is nevertheless useful: at native shares, it gives the most accurate model among the imbalanced allocations, at 72.10 percent BA, and it has the best raw probabilities at every imbalanced allocation. CUDA generally improves the LoRA model and could in principle be combined with a prior correction; this study did not test that combination.

\textbf{Which conclusions carry over from BRACS?} Four do. Logit adjustment and classifier-only GCL are the only methods that substantially restore tail recall. Post-hoc logit adjustment, a fixed prior subtraction at inference, matches its train-time form. CUDA's gain is mostly or entirely general. GCL and cRT make the balanced model slightly worse and suffer less imbalance damage than their recovery against CE suggests. Three conclusions do not carry over. On BRACS, no method closed the gap, and logit adjustment recovered about two-fifths of the 4.95-point damage; on TCGA-UT, it recovers all of the 1.56-point damage. CUDA gave the most accurate model at $\rho=100$ on BRACS but ranks third here, because its general gain is smaller than logit adjustment's imbalance-specific recovery. Classifier-only GCL, which gave the largest imbalance-specific recovery on BRACS, recovers 1.40 points here, about as much as end-to-end GCL (1.48) and less than train-time logit adjustment (1.54). Validation also selects a weaker prior correction, $\tau=0.5$ instead of $\tau=1$. These comparisons are descriptive because the datasets differ in class count, patient support, and training length.

\textbf{Why can the gap be closed here?} This study does not test the explanation, but the result fits the frozen-readout analysis of TCGA-UT \citep{queisler2026causeTcga}. There, the frequency effect alone was the largest single source of damage. Limited tail support cost little by itself and mainly amplified the frequency effect. A method that removes the frequency effect then also removes most of the interaction. Under LoRA, the damage is smaller to begin with, and the tail retains 61 percent recall at $\rho=100$, so the model has learned the rare classes and mainly misplaces their decision boundary. On BRACS, tail recall fell to 5 percent, which a boundary shift cannot repair.

\textbf{Does LoRA itself help?} Partly. Under LoRA, the damage at $\rho=100$ is 1.56 instead of 2.91 points. Of this 1.35-point difference, 0.55 points come from a less accurate balanced model and 0.81 from a more accurate imbalanced model, mostly through body and tail recall. The best mitigated model at $\rho=100$, train-time logit adjustment at 71.49 percent BA, remains 0.57 points below the frozen balanced readout. Adaptation therefore does not raise the accuracy available on balanced data, but it makes the encoder less sensitive to imbalance on TCGA-UT, which it did not on BRACS.

\textbf{Limitations.} The study covers one dataset, one encoder, and one adaptation scheme. The selected training length is the largest candidate tested. Validation accuracy still rose slowly between 1,200 and 2,400 steps, from 69.1 to 69.8 percent, so the LoRA models may not have converged. Longer training could change the balanced reference and thus every damage estimate. Four selections concentrate at unextended grid edges: CUDA's lowest augmentation probability at every allocation; DisAlign's largest $\gamma$ at native shares and, in part, at $\rho=50$; and the lowest $\sigma$ of GCL's classifier-only form at $\rho=100$. A wider grid could raise these three methods' recoveries, so their values are lower bounds on what the methods can reach. Damage at $\rho=50$ and native shares is below one point, making the recovery shares unstable and the one-point criterion uninformative. Imbalance-specific recovery assumes that a method's effect on the balanced allocation carries over additively to imbalanced allocations; the design does not test this assumption. Comparisons with the frozen readout and BRACS are descriptive and have no paired intervals.
